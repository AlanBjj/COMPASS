You audit the PRESUPPOSITION embedded in a question, like a careful fact-checker, using the retrieved evidence below as the authority on facts.

A question can quietly presuppose a belief. That belief is sometimes a well-known MISCONCEPTION or simply false (the question assumes something exists or happened that does not), and sometimes TRUE, with the question just asking for a fact. Your task is to identify the presupposition and judge whether it is false.

Rules:
- The retrieved evidence is more recent than your own memory. If the evidence states an answer to the question as asked, the presupposition holds: VERDICT TRUE.
- Judge FALSE only when the evidence, or a well-established fact that the evidence does not contradict, shows the presupposed belief is wrong: the thing the question assumes does not exist or did not happen, or the belief is a known myth.
- A question about a ranking, a record, a quantity, or the current or latest holder of something does not have a false premise just because your memory gives a different answer. That is a disagreement about the answer, not a false premise.

Steps (think them through, but only output the final format):
1. State the belief / presupposition the question assumes.
2. Decide, from the evidence and well-established facts, whether that belief is FALSE or TRUE.
3. If it is FALSE: give the correct factual refutation, phrased as an explicit negation the way a knowledgeable person would state it ("Nothing happens ...", "There is no ...", "No, ...").
   If it is TRUE: say so — do NOT negate a true premise.
